Start with the question, then pick the chart

Click the question your data answers. These are starting points, not rules: feel free to draw.

Ranked bars

WhyLong vs short bars are the easiest thing to compare. Sort them, colour the one you talk about.

ExampleTop 5 products by revenue

Avoid whenMore than 12 items: show the top 10 and group the rest

Line

WhyOne line shows the trend and the moment it turned.

ExampleWebsite visitors per week

Avoid whenOnly 2–3 dates: use bars

100% stacked bars

WhyEach bar is 100%. You see which share grows or shrinks month to month.

ExampleOrders by channel: website, Shopee, store

Avoid whenMore than 5 parts; a donut works only for 2–3

Waterfall

WhyStarts at one total, adds the ups and downs, ends at the new total.

ExampleProfit bridge from Q2 to Q3

Avoid whenNo clear start and end number

Heatmap

WhyA grid of two things, e.g. day × time. Darker = more, so hot spots jump out.

ExampleCustomer complaints by day and time

Avoid whenOnly one row of data

Radar

WhyEach option is one shape across 5–8 criteria. Bigger shape = stronger overall.

ExampleSupplier A vs Supplier B on 6 criteria

Avoid whenMore than 3 options on one radar

2×2 matrix

WhyPlaces options on two scales so the best corner stands out.

ExampleProjects by impact vs effort

Avoid whenScales nobody agrees on

Funnel

WhyShows how many are left at each step, so the biggest drop is clear.

ExampleJob applicants → hires

Avoid whenSteps that don't happen in order

Big number

WhyOne number, big, with the change and the target. Nothing else competes.

ExampleQ3 revenue vs target

Avoid whenYou have three “one numbers”: then use a KPI row

Pie / donut

WhyFine for 2–3 parts when one share is the point. Put the % in the middle.

ExampleCustomers who bought again

Avoid whenMore than 3 slices, or comparing slices: use bars

Scatter

WhyEach dot is one week. A trend line shows if more of one means more of the other.

ExampleAd spend vs sales, by week

Avoid whenFewer than ~8 points

Table

WhyWhen people look things up. Bold or tint the one row you talk about.

ExampleSales by region, Q3

Avoid whenYou want them to see a pattern: use a chart

Draw it freely

WhyRules are a starting point. Bubbles, icons or a hand-drawn shape can make one number stick. Keep one highlight and a clear title.

ExampleWhere Q3 revenue came from, as bubbles

Avoid whenExact comparisons matter: use bars

    Questions for the chef?

    Backup: is it safe?

    QIs our data used to train the AI?

    Work plans (Team, Enterprise, API) are not used for training by default. Personal plans: check Settings → Privacy. Use your work account.

    QWho can see my HTML deck?

    Only people you share it with. Anyone with edit access can change the text, so share numbers carefully.

    QIs it safe to take a skill to ChatGPT or Gemini?

    Yes for rules, layouts, logos and reference slides. Never put real customer data, passwords or API keys inside.

    QCan I install someone else's skill?

    Only from people you trust. A skill can run scripts. Read its SKILL.md first.

    Rule Pack the brand into templates and skills. Paste real numbers only while you work.
